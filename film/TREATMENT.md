# TREATMENT — "The Man on Somerton Beach" (working title)
## A Halftone Dossier documentary · target ≈ 15:00 · 1920×1080 · 24 fps · English

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. Chronological police procedural.** Dec 1948 → Jan 1949 → June 1949 → July 1949 → decades of
dead ends → 2022 DNA. Strength: clean, evidence-first. Weakness: can feel like a list; risks a flat
middle if every exhibit is treated with equal weight.

**B. Works-backwards from the name.** Open on 2022 — "we have a name" — then peel the file backwards,
asking what actually supports it. Strength: dramatic irony. Weakness: burns the biggest reveal in the
first 30 seconds and invites the audience to disengage from the historical evidence.

**C. The case file, read out one exhibit at a time (CHOSEN).** The film *is* a dossier that assembles
itself in front of the viewer: numbered exhibits, stamps, printed cards. Chronology inside the file
(discovery → investigation → paper → book → suitcase → theories → DNA → ledger → conclusion), with a
bureaucratic frame that makes the evidence-vs-speculation discipline *visible*: every claim is either
an **EXHIBIT** (hard evidence) or gets a red **SPECULATION** stamp. Ending: the file is filed in a
drawer of identical files — the mystery closes without being manufactured shut.

Chosen because the Halftone Dossier style is literally "a file that is read out one exhibit at a
time", and the user's central editorial rule — *never blur evidence and speculation* — becomes a
native visual mechanic of the style instead of an on-screen disclaimer.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline.** At dawn on 1 December 1948, a well-dressed man is found dead on Somerton Beach — no name,
no cause of death, and months later a torn slip of paper reading "Tamám Shud" — the complete file on
Australia's most profound mystery, read out one exhibit at a time.

**Arc.** Setup: a body nobody can name, in a city that tries everything. Turn: the Tamám Shud scrap —
the case stops being a drowning inquest and becomes a cipher. Ending: 2022 gives the file a *name*,
but not a *verdict* — the strongest evidence, the contradictions, and the questions that stay open;
the drawer closes on an OPEN stamp.

## 3. Benchmark (grammar only — never copy)

- **"The Keepers" (Netflix, 2016)** — what to learn: chaptered cold-case pacing, restraint before
  reveals, returning to one recurring image as an emotional anchor. What NOT to take: docu-footage
  texture, talking-head grammar, title design, music.
- **1940s newspaper front pages + film-noir title cards (e.g. "L.A. Confidential" opening montage)** —
  what to learn: headline hierarchy, dated card rhythm, the feeling that print itself carries
  authority. What NOT to take: specific headlines, photos, fonts, compositions.

## 8. Subtitles and title design

- **Narration subtitle = the caption bar** (style §4): dark-ink pill, paper text, keyword in highlight
  ink, overprint shadow; pops after headline; holds ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Synced from TTS
  word timings.
- `.srt` exported from the same cues (srt.py) for YouTube.
- **Headlines**: Playfair Display 900, 90–180 px, misregistration shadow +8/+8. **Numerals/chips**:
  Lilita One. **HUD/data**: JetBrains Mono 800. **Poetry/book pages**: IM Fell English.
- HUD: top-left case chip "CASE FILE 48/1948 · SOMERTON" + section chip "EXHIBIT 0n"; top-right REC
  dot + date stamp ("30 NOV 1948" etc., inverting on dark scenes).
- Palette: cream paper / black-brown ink (outlines, caption bars) / sepia-dark teal (overprint
  shadow) / dark navy night ground / mustard highlight / vermilion red reserved for stamps and
  SPECULATION. Five inks + paper, per style §3.

## 9. Factual-integrity rules for this film

1. Every visual "photograph" is a **labelled reconstruction** (tagged RECONSTRUCTION in the frame) —
   no fabricated archival imagery.
2. Quotes appear only where documented (Dwyer, Cleland, Leane, Coxon, the 1949 editorial).
3. Theories are always stamped SPECULATION; the Webb identification is always paired with
   "NOT OFFICIALLY VERIFIED".
4. Uncertain details (book-find timing, body-moved theory) are presented as the recorded
   contradictions, not resolved silently.
5. Source list ships with the film (SOURCES.md) and in the YouTube description.

## 10. Production plan (constrained environment)

2 CPUs / 3 GB RAM. Render the full timeline with `core/render/video.mjs --workers 2` (the tool
renders in per-worker segments and joins losslessly — the existing supported segmented workflow).
Run as a monitored background job; verify no lock conflicts. Music synthesized with numpy in the
style's palette (no sample download needed); voice via Kokoro offline (voice `bm_george`,
documentary-grave British narrator), ASR-checked line by line with word timings driving both the
caption bars and the SRT. Deliverables: `somerton-man.mp4`, `poster.jpg` (thumbnail),
`somerton-man.srt`, `TREATMENT.md`, `FACTS.md`, `SOURCES.md`, `CREDITS`, `build.sh`, YouTube
metadata (title/description/chapters) — published to the user's GitHub repo Release.
