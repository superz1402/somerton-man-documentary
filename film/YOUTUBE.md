## Description

At dawn on 1 December 1948, a well-dressed man was found dead on Somerton Beach, Adelaide —
sitting against the sea wall, feet crossed, a half-smoked cigarette on his collar. No wallet.
No identification. Not a single label on his clothes. Months later, sewn into a hidden fob
pocket, police found a rolled scrap of paper with two Persian words: Tamám Shud — "ended. It is
finished."

This 15½-minute investigative documentary reads the complete file one exhibit at a time: the
body and the autopsy that found no cause of death; the suitcase checked in the day before with
every tag removed except three spelling "Keane"; the copy of the Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam found
in an unlocked car on Jetty Road; the five lines of capital letters that have never been broken;
the nurse four hundred metres away who was "completely taken aback" by the plaster cast; the
decades of espionage and suicide theories — each stamped for what it really is; and the 2022
genetic-genealogy claim that the man was Carl "Charles" Webb — presented with its evidentiary
basis and its official caveats.

Every claim is sourced; evidence is separated on screen from speculation; the cause of death
was never determined, and this film does not pretend otherwise.
